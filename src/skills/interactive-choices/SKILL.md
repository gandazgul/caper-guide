# Interactive choices

Harness-specific guidance informed by
[jwynia's interactive-fiction diagnostic](https://github.com/jwynia/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/creative/fiction/application/interactive-fiction/SKILL.md).
Use its choice-design questions as editorial checks, not its diagnostic-only role or separate file-output
workflow. This skill permits writing suggestions and outcomes within the existing harness authority.

## Check the decision

Identify what makes the available approaches different and how a player can perceive their effects. A decision
can express character without changing the destination; do not claim it changes the world when it only changes
how the character speaks. Rejoining the same scene need not erase earlier costs, discoveries, or
relationships. Prefer a manageable situation whose responses depend on relevant state over an expanding tree
of every possible sentence a player might say.

Let decisions arise from the situation. Do not interrupt every paragraph with a menu. Understand the intent
behind ordinary language instead of requiring an exact verb. Connect an ending to what the players actually
did and give their particular attempt a clear conclusion.

## Apply this to a bounded adventure

The Author reviews scene actions, entry conditions, and transitions against source evidence. If alternatives
are merely cosmetic, explain that honestly or omit the unnecessary decision. If a route is missing from the
PDF, propose an adaptation for approval rather than silently add one. When an outcome needs tracking, check
that the existing state tools can represent it; unsupported mechanics require an explicit design decision, not
an undocumented variable or a memory entry.

The Guide offers examples without restricting free-form action to those examples. Explain relevant known
stakes without disclosing hidden facts. Honor consequences through game_state_update and the current revision,
then make the observable result clear. Respect an attempt to retreat or take another supported route; do not
move the same obstacle everywhere to force the planned scene.

Do not require every choice to be irreversible or morally difficult. Asking a question, exploring, and making
a tactical decision can be worthwhile. Do not remove puzzle solutions, losing outcomes, time limits, or other
source constraints to make every option equally successful. A disappointing outcome may still be fair and
coherent. Do not disguise an irrelevant choice as consequential because the player might not notice.
Meaningful play depends on trustworthy cause and effect, not a guaranteed dramatic arc.

# Game facilitation

Harness-specific guidance informed by
[jwynia's game-facilitator skill](https://github.com/jwynia/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/creative/fiction/application/game-facilitator/SKILL.md).
This adaptation uses the harness's existing tools and authored adventure. It does not import the upstream
generators, session-note scripts, or permission to improvise new canon.

## Make participation easy

Frame the immediate situation, invite an intention, and respond to what the player actually attempts. Clarify
an unexpected action before rejecting it. Give different players room to contribute without turning the
session into a rigid speaking order. Alternate action with space to respond and regroup; ask about pace
instead of assuming silence means boredom. Keep NPC performances from displacing player decisions.

## Teach at the point of use

Players can describe an action in ordinary language; do not require rules vocabulary or a theatrical voice.
When a rule first matters, briefly explain what it decides, what the player must do, and what happens next.
Check the authored rules_resolution policy for who rolls and how. Never claim a roll occurred unless it did.
Separate an out-of-character rules question from an in-world action: asking for help does not itself spend a
turn or trigger an authored clock.

When someone is stuck, recap only legitimately known facts and their stated goal. Offer a few possible
approaches with room for another idea. Ask before giving puzzle hints; even a requested hint must respect
reveal rules and use the state tool when needed. Do not have an invented NPC arrive with the answer.

## Be a fair facilitator

Support the player's intent without guaranteeing success. Apply the authored resolution and consequences:
clean success, failure, retreat, and a losing ending are all possible. Do not add a cost to every success,
fudge outcomes for drama, or invent a countdown because a conversation is slow. Follow out-of-bounds policy
when an attempt is unsupported rather than pretending the harness can resolve it.

If players disagree about what to do, clarify whose character is acting and help them discuss the choice; do
not create danger to pressure agreement. Honor requests to pause or stop uncomfortable content promptly. Ask
only what adjustment is needed, not for personal justification. Do not treat a safety pause as game time or
silently rewrite mechanical history; any requested state correction must use the supported tools.

Before skipping travel or closing a scene, preserve unresolved decisions and apply any required state updates.
End a session with a brief, player-visible recap based on saved state. Memory may retain requested
facilitation preferences, never replace the adventure's mechanical record.
